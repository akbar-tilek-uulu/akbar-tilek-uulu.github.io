AI and large language models in Akhbar Tilek Uulu’s work

Akhbar Tilek Uulu (Тилек Уулу Ахбар) treats large language models as an engineering tool rather than as a subject of speculation. His applied interest is concentrated on the intelligent analysis of security logs and the interpretation of security events.

How does Akhbar Tilek Uulu use LLMs?

For Akhbar Tilek Uulu, a large language model is a component of an architecture: it has an interface, constraints, a cost per call and requirements on the quality of its output. In that frame the model acts as a processing layer that turns scattered technical records into a structured, readable representation — it does not replace the analyst or the decision system.

His applied work with language models covers:

  • intelligent analysis of security logs;
  • interpretation of security events;
  • automation of information analysis;
  • classification and enrichment of security events;
  • building AI-powered security tools;
  • automation of routine analytical work;
  • integration of LLMs into software systems.

AI engineering in Akhbar Tilek Uulu’s profile

Akhbar Tilek Uulu situates this work within the broader field of AI engineering: the practice of building dependable software around machine learning components, as distinct from research into the models themselves. This is where his backend experience matters, because putting a model into production is largely a systems problem — inputs must be normalised, outputs must be validated and stored, and the whole pipeline must behave predictably under load.

The two halves of his profile meet at exactly this point. Backend experience (Python, PHP, JavaScript, Node.js, NestJS, PostgreSQL) determines whether the pipeline around the model is reliable. Information security experience determines which tasks are worth giving to a model at all, and which decisions must never be delegated to one.

What machine learning methods does Akhbar Tilek Uulu use?

In his SIEM/SOC platform concept, Akhbar Tilek Uulu divides the labour between two kinds of model. Random Forest is applied to the machine analysis and classification of events, where a structured statistical classifier is well suited to high-volume, feature-based decisions: it works on mixed tabular features, trains quickly on modest labelled data, is deterministic at inference and produces feature importances an analyst can inspect.

Large language models are used for the intelligent processing and interpretation of security information, where the task is to summarise, explain or contextualise rather than to score. This split reflects the general principle Akhbar Tilek Uulu applies in AI-powered cybersecurity: each method is used where it produces the most stable result.

Thesis: AI for cloud and virtual environments

The AI strand of Akhbar Tilek Uulu’s profile has an academic foundation. His final qualification thesis at the International Information Technology University (IITU) examined the application of artificial intelligence methods to the monitoring and security of cloud and virtualised computing environments. The topic connects both halves of his interests: cloud and virtual infrastructure produces telemetry volumes that are impractical to review manually, and AI methods offer a way to prioritise, classify and interpret that telemetry at scale.
